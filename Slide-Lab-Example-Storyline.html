Dot-dash storyline: Southeast Asia Entry

Deck type
Business Case
Governing thought
Meridian should enter Southeast Asia through Vietnam first, then Indonesia, starting with $12M of Phase 1 funding.
Audience
Meridian's investment committee. They see Southeast Asia as attractive but risky, and lean toward either a large multi-market launch or waiting.
Belief to break
"Entering Southeast Asia means a big, all-at-once bet across the region."
Belief to leave with
"A capped $12M first step in Vietnam, with two stop points, tells us whether to scale before we commit more."
Room should say back
"Approve $12M for Vietnam now; decide on Indonesia at month 18."
Read the dots top-to-bottom — they should form the deck's argument as a single coherent story. If the dots-alone don't make sense, the storyline is broken.
Slide 1 — Southeast Asia entry: Vietnam first
Meridian should enter Southeast Asia through Vietnam first, then Indonesia.
Slide 2 — Leading with Vietnam reaches a $40M run-rate in 18 months for $12M
Leading with Vietnam reaches a $40M revenue run-rate in 18 months for $12M.
  • Vietnam, then Indonesia: the only two of five markets that clear both the $1.0B market-size bar and the 3.5 readiness bar.
  • The regional category doubles from $2.1B in 2024 to $4.2B in 2030, and Vietnam passes Thailand as a market in 2027.
  • A five-step, partner-led playbook, proven in one city before national rollout, then reused in Indonesia.
  • $12M for Phase 1, capped until Gate 2, for a $40M revenue run-rate within 18 months.
  • Thailand first fails the size bar ($0.8B by 2030); launching in all five at once spreads $12M too thin to prove anything.
  • Approve $12M of Phase 1 funding by 15 Nov 2026; decide on Indonesia at month 18.
  • Takeaway – The committee is not being asked to bet on the region; it is being asked to fund one capped, gated test that pays back fast if it works and stops early if it does not.
Slide 3 — The Southeast Asia market doubles to $4.2B by 2030
The Southeast Asia category market doubles to $4.2B by 2030.
  • The category grows from $2.1B in 2024 to $4.2B in 2030, about 12% a year.
  • Vietnam (18% a year) and Indonesia (15% a year) grow fastest and make up $2.5B of the 2030 total.
  • Thailand (6%) and Malaysia (5%) grow at under half the regional rate.

  • Takeaway – This is a growth market worth a dedicated entry, not an export side-line; waiting means entering later, into a market twice the size.
Slide 4 — Only Vietnam and Indonesia clear both the size and readiness bar
Only Vietnam and Indonesia clear both the size and the readiness bar.
  • A market must reach at least $1.0B by 2030 and score at least 3.5 out of 5 on readiness (partners, distribution, rules for foreign brands).
  • $1.2B by 2030, 18% a year, readiness 4.0 (partners 4, distribution 4, rules 4).
  • $1.3B by 2030, 15% a year, readiness 3.7 (partners 4, distribution 4, rules 3).
  • Ready (4.0) but only $0.8B by 2030, growing 6% a year.
  • $0.5B and $0.4B by 2030, readiness 2.7 and 3.3.
  • Takeaway – The choice of market is not a judgment call: two markets pass a simple, explicit screen and three fail it.
Slide 5 — Vietnam passes Thailand in 2027 and the gap keeps widening
Vietnam passes Thailand as a market in 2027 and the gap keeps widening.
  • In 2024 Thailand is the larger market, $564M against Vietnam's $444M.
  • Vietnam reaches $729M in 2027, passing Thailand's $672M.
  • Vietnam reaches $1,200M against Thailand's $800M.
  • Similar size, but Vietnam grows faster (18% against 15% a year) and is more ready (4.0 against 3.7), so it is the safer first proof.
  • Exhibit: line chart — (data table in brief)
  • Takeaway – Choosing by today's market size picks the wrong country; by 2030 Vietnam is 1.5 times Thailand.
Slide 6 — A five-step playbook takes each market from partner search to scale
A five-step playbook takes each market from partner search to scale.
  • Choose and sign one local distribution partner with reach into modern and traditional trade.
  • Launch in Ho Chi Minh City first and track sell-through against target.
  • Scale only if the pilot meets its sell-through target; otherwise stop or fix.
  • Roll out across the country with the partner once the unit economics hold.
  • Run the same five steps in Indonesia, starting with what Vietnam taught.qualitative
  • Takeaway – Entry is a repeatable process with a proof point before every big spend, so Indonesia reuses what Vietnam teaches instead of starting over.
Slide 7 — The 18-month plan has two go/no-go gates before spending scales
The 18-month plan has two go/no-go gates before spending scales.
  • Choose and sign the Vietnam distribution partner.
  • Pilot in Ho Chi Minh City.
  • Continue only if pilot sell-through is at or above target.
  • National rollout in Vietnam.
  • Continue only if contribution margin is at least 15%.
  • Indonesia partner search and entry preparation, with the Phase 2 funding decision at month 18.
  • Takeaway – The committee keeps control: there are two dated points where it can stop before most of the money is spent.
Slide 8 — Local partners power a growth loop that makes each next market cheaper
Local partners power a growth loop that makes each next market cheaper.
  • Each market adds a distribution partner and its retailer relationships.
  • More outlets carry Meridian products sooner.
  • Wider distribution gives earlier, more detailed sell-through data.
  • The data shows which products, prices and channels work.
  • The next market starts from proven choices, so it costs less to enter, which attracts more partners.qualitative
  • Takeaway – The first market is the expensive one; partners turn one success into a cheaper entry to the next.
Slide 9 — Spending stays capped at $12M until Gate 2 proves the unit economics
Spending stays capped at $12M until Gate 2 proves the unit economics.
  • $4.0M, a third of the total.
  • Partner onboarding $2.5M and distribution set-up $3.0M.
  • A local team of $1.5M and $1.0M of contingency.
  • Indonesia is a new funding decision at month 18, not part of this $12M.
  • Exhibit: waterfall chart — (data table in brief)
  • Takeaway – The downside is known and bounded: the most Meridian can spend before the second stop point is $12M, and every part of it is named.
Slide 10 — Approve $12M of Phase 1 funding by 15 November 2026
Approve $12M of Phase 1 funding by 15 November 2026.
  • Meridian waits; the category doubles by 2030 and Vietnam passes Thailand in 2027 without us.
  • Takeaway – One decision today starts the clock; every later commitment comes back to the committee at a gate.
