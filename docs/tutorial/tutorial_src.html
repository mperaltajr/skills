Slide Lab: what to do, step by step, and what to expect from Claude

Slide Lab is a set of add-ons for Claude (the AI assistant you chat with in the Claude desktop app) that builds a client-branded PowerPoint deck with you. You bring the message, the audience, your notes and the client's PowerPoint template; Claude helps shape the story, designs every slide on that template, lets you pick the designs, checks the result and hands you a normal, editable .pptx file.

Read section 1 for the whole picture in under 3 minutes. Use section 4 when you are in the middle of a deck and want to know what happens next, how long it takes and what to do if something goes wrong.

Accurate as of (Slide Lab version , the code's short ID).
Newest copy: always in your Slide Lab folder, C:\Users\<you>\.claude\skills\Slide-Lab-Tutorial.html, updated when Slide Lab updates. A copy someone emailed you may be out of date.
The running example. Throughout this guide we follow one real Slide Lab build: a 10-slide deck called "Meridian: Southeast Asia entry". Meridian is a made-up company; its template and every number are invented. The timings in this guide were measured on that build. You can open its files, which sit in the same folder as this guide: the storyline, the review page (the real first-round page, options per slide) and the finished deck.

4.3Shape the storyline

Before any slide exists, Claude coaches the argument. The result is a dot-dash storyline: one bold claim per slide (the dot) with its supporting points (the dashes). Read the dots top to bottom and they should tell the whole story. Claude writes each headline as a fact the audience can check, with a number where there is one ("Only Vietnam and Indonesia clear both the size and the readiness bar"), not a slogan ("Vietnam is the clear winner"). Claude also saves a brief, the detailed plan the slide designs are built from.

4.8Build and quality check

The quality check opens a picture of every slide and reads it area by area, looking for text that overflows, type that is too small, things out of line and leftover placeholder text. Each finding gets a severity: Critical (must be fixed), Major (fix it, or give your reason to keep it) or Advisory (a suggestion, never blocks). Claude does not call a deck done while a Critical or Major finding is open.

10Common questions

Do I need to know how to code or design?
No. You talk to Claude in plain English and click buttons on two web pages. Claude asks before it runs anything on your computer; click Allow when it does.
What if I don't like any of the designs for a slide?
Click REPLACE THESE and say in the notes what you want different. You get new design by default; say "with 3 options" if you want alternatives. For a small change, pick the closest option and add a quick-feedback button or a note instead.
Why do I paste a message back into Claude?
The review pages are files on your computer and cannot talk to Claude directly. The copied message carries your exact choices with a check code, so nothing is retyped or misread. Paste it unchanged.
Can I keep my work in OneDrive?
Keep the session folder on your computer's own drive (C:\Users\<you>\Slide Lab\sessions\). In OneDrive, builds are noticeably slower and previews can time out. Copy the finished deck to OneDrive or SharePoint afterwards. The template itself can live anywhere.
Can I edit the charts afterwards?
Charts are drawn from shapes for a clean, on-brand look, so they are not linked to a datasheet. Either ask Claude to rebuild the slide with new numbers, or say "Export the chart data to Excel" and paste the sheet into think-cell or a normal PowerPoint chart.
Is my client's template safe?
Yes. Slide Lab works on its own copy and never changes your original file. Your work stays on your computer.
Can it work on a deck I already have?
Yes, "Change a deck" in the menu: fix text or numbers, redesign a slide and drop it back into your deck, or refresh a recurring deck with this cycle's text. It always writes a new file.
Does it work on a Mac?
Yes. The install guide covers Windows and Mac. Paths look different on a Mac (your home folder instead of C:\Users\<you>).
