# Acme Q3 Churn — Strategic Pivot

## Governing thought (the whole deck)
Acme should treat the Q3 customer-churn spike as a product-fit signal, not a marketing-spend gap, and reallocate the next two quarters of growth budget to onboarding and activation.

## Audience
Acme Corp executive team. Currently believe quarterly churn requires bigger marketing spend; deck must convince them the spike is product-fit, not awareness.

**Audience assumption to break:** "Q3 churn is a marketing problem — we need to spend more on brand."
**Audience belief to leave with:** "Churn is concentrated in the first 14 days. The fix is the onboarding flow, not the funnel top."
**The single sentence the room should say back:** "Reallocate $4M from Q4 brand to onboarding redesign."

### Slide 2 — Churn jumped 38% in Q3 — but the cause isn't where you'd expect

**Slide type:** Synthesis / Findings

**Governing thought (the claim):** 72% of Q3 churn cancelled within 14 days of signup — the failure point is onboarding, not renewal.

**The takeaway:** Acme's instinct is to escalate marketing spend. The data argues the opposite: churn is concentrated in the first 14 days, which means the product-onboarding flow is the failure point.

**Editorial emphasis:** the numbers — 38%, 72%, and the flat 6-month cohort are the load-bearing claims.

**Evidence / content:**

- **38% YoY CHURN SPIKE** — Monthly logo churn jumped 38% year-over-year in Q3, the steepest single-quarter increase since Acme's 2022 reset.
- **72% CANCEL WITHIN 14 DAYS** — Of churned accounts, 72% cancelled within 14 days of signup. The matching engine isn't broken; new users never activate.
- **6-MONTH COHORTS FLAT** — Renewal cohorts older than 6 months show flat retention. Existing customers are sticky; the problem is acquisition-to-activation.

**Slide type:** Recommendation

**Governing thought (the claim):** The brand-spend path buys one quarter of cover; the product-investment path permanently closes the gap.

**The takeaway:** Doubling down on brand compounds the underlying defect. The product-investment path is two quarters slower but permanently fixes the activation problem.

**Editorial emphasis:** the contrast — Path A vs Path B side-by-side, with Path B's permanent fix dominating.

**Evidence / content:**

- **PATH A · BRAND SPEND** — $4M into Q4 campaigns. Buys 1 quarter of churn cover. Cost compounds; activation defect persists into Q1.
- **PATH B · PRODUCT INVESTMENT** — $4M into onboarding redesign + activation tooling. 2-quarter payback. Permanent fix to the 14-day cliff.
