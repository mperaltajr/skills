# reading-order-bottom-up — WHY NOT

**Family:** Insight / Finding
**Verdict:** dont

## The problem

The slide's hero takeaway — the load-bearing claim the whole page exists
to deliver — sits in the BOTTOM strip of the body zone. The TOP and MIDDLE
zones are occupied by supporting evidence: a dense context paragraph
followed by a chart placeholder.

Readers scan top-to-bottom (and left-to-right). They expect the headline
claim first, the evidence under it. On this slide they get the inverse:

1. A paragraph of methodology / context with no framing claim
2. A chart they have to interpret cold, without knowing what to look for
3. The actual point of the slide — only if they make it that far

Most readers stop somewhere in step 1 or step 2. The slide buries its own
argument. Worse, the reader has to mentally backtrack — re-read the
evidence in light of the late-arriving claim — to make the slide work at
all. That's an eye-jump tax the consultant should never charge.

## Rule it breaks

**Reading order is top-to-bottom or left-to-right; the takeaway / headline
lives in the top zone (immediately after the title block); supporting
evidence flows below or to the right; never place the takeaway below the
evidence.**

The title block at the top of the page does not satisfy this rule. The
title is the slide's *topic label* ("[Slide title: topic of the analysis]");
the *takeaway* is the load-bearing claim that earns the slide its place in
the deck. The takeaway must sit at the top of the body zone, directly under
the title block. Evidence supports it from below.

## Why this is a teaching anti-exemplar

This failure mode is common in junior consultant work and in slides
auto-generated from "show the data first" instincts. It looks balanced
visually — title up top, hero claim at the bottom, evidence sandwiched
between — but it fails the comprehension test the moment the slide is
read in real time (a stand-up, a printed leave-behind, a 10-second skim
in a board pack).

It's a clean teaching example because:
- The inversion is unambiguous (top = evidence, bottom = claim)
- It's easy to "fix" by flipping the zones — making the rule concrete
- It's a failure of *editorial* judgment, not visual craft: the slide
  could be beautifully styled and still fail this rule

## What to do instead

Put the hero takeaway at the TOP of the body zone (y ≈ 152–280),
immediately under the title block. Let supporting evidence — paragraphs,
charts, callouts, bars — flow BELOW it (y ≈ 290–640).

Readers should hit the claim first, the evidence second. The evidence
exists to defend a claim the reader has already absorbed.

The inverted (correct) reading-order pattern is claim or KPI on top,
evidence under it, optional one-line so-what strip at the bottom
restating the claim — never introducing it. See `reference/layouts.md`
§ "Full canvas" and § "Top band + body" for the canonical patterns
that implement this reading order.
