# Stage A Protocol Review — Reviewer B

**Angle:** measurement validity + confound control.
**Question:** if Stage A produces a number, will that number mean anything?
**Date:** 2026-05-25
**Prior position:** I raised the "no v1-NOW baseline exists" point that motivated Stage A. I am now reviewing my own proposed gate. My job here is to refuse a clean-looking number that is actually meaningless.

---

## 0. The honest framing of what Stage A is doing

The prior committee concluded: "re-measure v1-NOW on slidelab-intro, compare to 23%, decide whether v2 is justified." That sentence contains at least four measurement assumptions that need to be named before any number is collected:

1. The 23% was a real number generated by a real methodology that can be re-applied.
2. slidelab-intro is a valid instrument to re-apply that methodology against.
3. "v1-NOW acceptance %" and "v1-AT-23% acceptance %" are comparable on the same axis.
4. The delta between them tells you something about whether v2 is justified.

**At least two of those four are false as currently scoped.** Stage A will produce a number. The question is whether that number licenses a consolidation decision or just provides cover for one.

---

## 1. Is comparing v1-NOW slidelab-intro acceptance % to the historical 23% valid?

**No. The corpus differs, the methodology was never written down with enough specificity to replicate, and slidelab-intro is structurally biased.**

Three concrete validity problems with the direct comparison:

**(a) Corpus non-equivalence.** The historical 23% was measured against whatever real client decks Mario was building when the number was generated — OTC findings, FedEx engagements, ACN internal decks. slidelab-intro is a synthetic regression brief engineered around v1''s failure modes (this is exactly Reviewer C''s point from the prior committee, and it cuts here too). Acceptance % on a brief designed to surface v1 failures is a *lower bound on v1 quality*, not a representative estimate. v1-NOW on slidelab-intro could land at 30% and v1-NOW on a real OTC brief could land at 55%. The number you collect is not the number you think you collected.

**(b) Methodology drift.** The 23% was a "curator acceptance / first-pass-reject" number. What counted as a reject in the v1-at-23% era? Was it "slide unusable as-is"? "Slide needs >5 min of rework"? "Slide structurally wrong vs brief"? Each of those is a different threshold and produces a different denominator. Without the original rubric written down, Mario re-running the methodology *is* the rubric — which means the comparison is "Mario''s 2025 judgment of v1-old" vs "Mario''s 2026 judgment of v1-new" on a different brief, with rubric drift between the two scoring events. That is not a like-for-like measurement.

**(c) Hindsight contamination.** Mario knows v2 exists. Mario knows v2 was built because v1 was at 23%. If v1-NOW lands at 40%, the unspoken question becomes "is 40% high enough to justify keeping v2 alive?" rather than "what does 40% mean?" The threshold is implicit and post-hoc. That is the exact "ceremony to manufacture conviction" pattern Reviewer C named.

**Right comparator:** the right comparator is not 23%. It is **"can v1-NOW ship a slidelab-intro deck that Mario would put in front of a partner without edits."** Binary, per-deck (not per-slide), one deck. If yes, v2''s justification has to come from somewhere other than "v1 is broken on the regression brief." If no, the failure mode itself is the signal — not the %.

The 23% historical number should be treated as **legacy context, not a comparator.** Quote it in DECISIONS.md as "v1 was at 23% in late-2025 on real-client work; that was the trigger for v2." Do not re-derive a 2026 number and pretend it is the same axis.

## 2. Does Stage A need capability-bucketing the way Stage B does?

**Yes, but for a different reason and at lower fidelity.**

Stage B (the formal v1-vs-v2) needs capability-bucketing because the asymmetric refusal capability (SKELETON_REJECTED, FALLBACK_MERMAID) creates a measurement inversion if you score "which PNG wins" without it. That problem exists at Stage B because v2 is on the stage.

Stage A has v1 alone. There is no SKELETON_REJECTED event possible — v1 will build something on every slide. So the *asymmetry* problem disappears. But a different bucketing problem appears in its place: **fabrication detection.**

If v1-NOW lands at "45% acceptance" on slidelab-intro, you need to know whether the 55% rejected slides were rejected because:
- The slide was ugly but content-correct (cosmetic failure — fixable by theme rewrite, possibly already fixed).
- The slide was visually fine but structurally wrong vs brief (chassis-pick failure).
- The slide *fabricated content not in the brief* (the slide-9 fabrication bug, the original motivating defect for v2).

**These three failure modes are not interchangeable.** A v1-NOW that fails 55% on cosmetics is a v1-NOW that the brand.yml theme rewrite probably already fixed and nobody re-measured. A v1-NOW that fails 55% on fabrication is the *exact* failure mode v2 was built to prevent and would justify v2 categorically regardless of the %.

**Required bucketing for Stage A (lighter than Stage B):**

| Bucket | Definition | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **Cosmetic-reject** | Slide rejected on visual quality alone; content is faithful to brief | Probably theme-rewrite-fixable; not a v2 justification |
| **Structural-reject** | Slide picked the wrong chassis for the content; brief preserved but mis-framed | Pattern vocabulary issue; could go either way on v2 justification |
| **Fabrication-reject** | Slide invented content not in the brief OR omitted content the brief required | Categorical v2 justification regardless of overall % |
| **Accept** | Mario would ship this slide to a partner with <5 min edits | Counts toward acceptance % |

Without this split, Stage A produces a number with no causal handle. *With* this split, even a "55% v1-NOW acceptance" result is decisively interpretable — if 30% of the rejects are fabrication, v2 is justified categorically; if 0% are fabrication, v2''s justification has to come from somewhere else.

The split is cheap: Mario is already looking at each rejected slide. Tagging it with one of four labels adds maybe 30 seconds per slide.

## 3. Confound risk if Mario scores his own work

This is the hardest constraint. No independent scorer is available. So the confound must be controlled procedurally rather than via a second judge.

The dominant confound risks when Mario is the only scorer:

**(a) Outcome anticipation.** Mario knows what number "should" come out. If he expects v1-NOW to land roughly where v1-old did, he will (unconsciously) score consistently with that. If he expects v1-NOW to be better because of theme rewrite, he will score more leniently.

**(b) Rubric drift mid-run.** Without a written rubric pinned before scoring starts, Mario''s "acceptance threshold" can shift across the 10 slides of slidelab-intro. By slide 7, "would I ship this?" can quietly relax into "would I ship this if I were tired and the deadline was tomorrow?" — which is a different question.

**(c) Aesthetic recency bias.** Whatever Mario saw most recently as "good" (a recent v2 smoke, a Slide Lab gallery image) becomes the implicit comparison standard, even when the explicit comparison is "v1-NOW against itself."

**(d) Sunk-cost bias on v2.** v2 exists. Mario built v2. If v1-NOW looks too good, the implicit cost of admitting v2 was unnecessary is real. This is the bias that matters most because it points the same direction as outcome anticipation and is hardest to detect.

**Mitigations that do not require a second scorer:**

1. **Pre-commit the rubric in writing, in this document, before any scoring.** A 4-line rubric (accept = ship to partner with <5 min edits; cosmetic-reject = visual quality only; structural-reject = wrong chassis; fabrication-reject = invented or dropped brief content). Pin it. Reference it during scoring. Do not amend mid-run.

2. **Score the slides in reverse brief order.** Slide 10 first, slide 1 last. This breaks the narrative-arc carryover where the first 2-3 slides set Mario''s threshold for the rest. Each slide is judged closer to in-isolation.

3. **Score from PNGs alone with a covered brief column.** No reading the brief side-by-side during the visual judgment. Brief gets consulted *after* the per-slide accept/cosmetic-reject/structural-reject call, only for the fabrication-reject check. This forces visual-quality scoring not to leak into content-fidelity scoring or vice versa.

4. **Inter-rater check with past-Mario.** Mario has scored slides before — there are existing curator decisions in the slide-builder exemplars/do and exemplars/dont folders. Before scoring slidelab-intro, Mario should score 3 of his own historical do/ exemplars and 3 dont/ exemplars *blind* (without knowing which is which) using the same rubric. If past-Mario and present-Mario don''t agree on at least 5 of 6, the rubric is not calibrated and Stage A data will be noisy. This is the closest substitute for a second scorer available — it''s Mario as his own control.

5. **Write the per-slide judgment in one sentence before recording the verdict.** "This slide is cosmetic-reject because the accent bar overlaps the icon and the type hierarchy is inverted." Forces the reason to be articulated before the label is locked. Without this, the label can be retrofitted to the desired aggregate %.

6. **Record the timestamp on each slide judgment.** If slides 1-5 take 4 minutes each and slides 6-10 take 45 seconds each, the latter five are fatigue-judgments and need to be redone. This is the scoring-fatigue mitigation Reviewer A flagged for Stage B; it applies in miniature here.

**The single highest-leverage mitigation is #1 + #5 together.** Written rubric, written per-slide reason, both pinned before the aggregate is computed. Everything else is secondary.

## 4. Decision rule for the ambiguous case (40-55% range)

This is the case the protocol must define explicitly because it is the most likely outcome and the most dangerous one to leave underspecified.

**Pre-commit, before scoring starts:**

| v1-NOW slidelab-intro acceptance | Decision |
|---|---|
| **>= 65%** (clear improvement vs 23%) | v1 is materially recovered. v2''s justification must come from capability (SKELETON_REJECTED / FALLBACK_MERMAID) or from delivering on briefs v1-NOW still fails on. Stage B becomes a narrower question: "is v2 justified for its asymmetric capabilities, given that v1-NOW is broadly competent?" Likely outcome: route by capability (fabrication-prone briefs to v2, everything else to v1-NOW). |
| **40-64%** (ambiguous) | **The default decision is v2 is justified, *unless* the fabrication-reject bucket from sec.2 is 0%.** Justification: v1-NOW at 40-64% is materially better than 23% but is still rejecting 1 in 3 to 1 in 2 slides, which is not "production-grade" by Mario''s own bar. v2 was built to address that gap. If the gap is real (fabrication present), v2 stays. If the gap is purely cosmetic (no fabrication, no structural errors), then the theme rewrite probably closes it and v2 is *not* categorically justified — go to Stage B to confirm. |
| **<= 39%** (no material improvement) | v1 is still at 23%-class behavior. v2 is justified categorically. Stage B is unnecessary — just consolidate to v2 and treat v1 as legacy. |

**Why the default-to-v2 in the ambiguous band:** the constraint says "the whole point is to know whether v2 is justified." In the ambiguous band, the cost of incorrectly killing v2 (losing the asymmetric refusal capability, losing the simpler 14-pattern vocabulary, losing the work invested) is higher than the cost of incorrectly keeping v2 (parallel-skill maintenance burden, which is bounded and visible). Asymmetric costs justify a default-to-v2 tiebreaker in the ambiguous band.

**The decision rule above is locked.** It is written here before any number is collected so that the post-hoc temptation to slide the threshold ("v1-NOW landed at 47%, that''s close enough to 50%, let''s call it ambiguous-favors-v1") is structurally prevented. If Mario wants to change the decision rule after seeing the number, the change must be made in writing with a separate signed rationale.

## 5. Cheaper proxy at 80% signal / 20% cost?

**Yes — and it is what should actually run first.**

The full Stage A as currently scoped costs ~2 hours of Mario time (build slidelab-intro through v1-NOW, score 10 slides with rubric, bucket the rejects). The cheaper proxy costs ~15 minutes and answers most of the question.

**Proxy: the "ship test" on the existing v1-NOW slidelab-intro deck.**

A v1-NOW slidelab-intro deck may already exist from prior smoke testing. If not, the build itself is ~10 minutes of compute (not Mario time). Then:

1. Mario opens the deck in PowerPoint.
2. Single binary judgment per slide: **"would I send this to a partner as-is?"** Yes / no. No bucketing, no rubric, no rejection-reason tagging.
3. Aggregate: yes-count / 10.

This is the curator-acceptance metric in its simplest form. It is exactly the methodology that produced the 23% number (probably — see methodology drift problem in sec.1) and is the most defensible re-application of it. It takes ~5 minutes of scoring.

**Then, only if the proxy lands in the 40-64% ambiguous band, run the full Stage A** with rubric and bucketing to decide between the two ambiguous-band paths.

This is a sequential gate: cheap test first, expensive test only when the cheap test does not resolve.

**Total cost in the common case:** 15 minutes of Mario time (build is free; scoring is ~5 min; review is ~5 min; doc write-up is ~5 min). **Cost in the ambiguous-band case:** 15 min + the full ~2 hours.

**What you lose vs the full Stage A:** the fabrication-vs-cosmetic-vs-structural bucketing is collapsed into binary ship/no-ship. You don''t get the fabrication-reject signal that would categorically justify v2. **Mitigation:** spend 2 extra minutes after the binary pass to mark which rejected slides were fabrication-flavored. That recovers the categorical-v2 signal at near-zero marginal cost.

**This is what I would actually run.** The full Stage A as drafted is over-instrumented for a question that has high prior probability of resolving cleanly on the binary measure alone.

## 6. Single highest-risk failure mode of running Stage A

**The number lands at exactly 50%, Mario decides to "look more carefully," and Stage A silently becomes Stage A.1, A.2, A.3 — the lazy-defer pattern at the meta-level.**

This is the worst failure mode because it is the most likely one and the hardest to detect from inside. The mechanism:

1. v1-NOW on slidelab-intro produces 5 accepts, 5 rejects.
2. Mario looks at the 5 rejects, notices that 2 are "marginal" — slides he would maybe accept with a small edit.
3. The temptation is "let me re-score with a slightly different threshold to see if it''s actually 7/10."
4. Re-scoring with a different threshold *is* changing the rubric mid-run.
5. The number ratchets toward whichever direction Mario already preferred.
6. The decision is made on a number that has been retro-calibrated and is no longer measuring what it claimed to measure.

This is more dangerous than scoring fatigue because it produces a confident-looking number, not a noisy one. The output of Stage A under this failure mode is *less* useful than no measurement at all because it carries the rhetorical weight of a measurement.

**Specific mitigation:**

**The rubric and the decision rule (sec.4) are both written into this document BEFORE Mario scores. Then:**

1. Mario scores all 10 slides on the proxy (sec.5) in one sitting, no breaks. ~5 minutes total. Each slide gets a binary verdict and a one-sentence reason in the same sentence: "Slide 4: REJECT — accent bar misaligned with title baseline."

2. **The aggregate is computed once.** It is recorded in this `_decisions/` folder with the timestamp. There is no second scoring pass on the same deck. There is no "let me look again." There is no "let me re-do slide 6." If Mario wants to re-score, the entire deck gets re-scored in a fresh session, the new number is recorded as a separate measurement, and *both* are reported. Two numbers from two scorings is honest; one number that quietly replaced an earlier one is fraudulent.

3. The decision rule from sec.4 is applied mechanically to the recorded number. If the number is 47%, that''s the ambiguous band, default-to-v2, done. If Mario wants to override the default, that override is written separately with a signed rationale — not folded back into "well, let''s just call it 52%."

4. **If after one scoring pass Mario feels he cannot decide, the protocol''s answer is: default to v2** (per sec.4). The decision is forced. No "let me build a second brief through v1-NOW to be sure." That is Stage A becoming Stage A++ becoming forever.

The structural prevention is: **one scoring event, one number, one decision rule applied mechanically.** Everything that adds a second scoring event, a re-scored slide, or a "let me also try" is the failure mode in motion.

## 7. What would actually answer the question Stage A is trying to answer?

The constraint says: "the whole point is to know whether v2 is justified. If your protocol can''t answer that, name what would."

**Stage A on slidelab-intro cannot fully answer whether v2 is justified, even with the mitigations above.** It can answer one narrower question: "has v1-NOW closed the gap on its own regression brief?" That is necessary but not sufficient.

What would actually answer "is v2 justified":

1. The sec.5 proxy run, giving a v1-NOW slidelab-intro acceptance number.
2. A 5-minute review of the v2 smoke artifacts already in `_decisions/` to count: how many SKELETON_REJECTED events fired correctly on slidelab-intro? How many FALLBACK_MERMAID events? If those events exist and represent fabrication-prone briefs v1 would have built-anyway-with-invented-content, then v2 is justified categorically by capability, *regardless* of the slidelab-intro acceptance %.
3. A single honest written paragraph from Mario: "the slide-9 fabrication bug that motivated v2 — does it still occur in v1-NOW or did the theme rewrite incidentally close it?" If v1-NOW still fabricates, v2 is justified. If v1-NOW does not fabricate (testable by running the trigger-brief from the smoke through v1-NOW), v2''s justification is much narrower.

**The combination of (1) + (2) + (3) answers the question in ~30 minutes.** The full multi-day Stage A + Stage B does not answer it more honestly; it just produces more numbers.

## TL;DR

Stage A as currently sketched is at risk of producing a clean-looking number that confounds three different things (cosmetic vs structural vs fabrication failure modes) and gets compared to a historical 23% it is not actually commensurate with. The fix is not more measurement — it is **less measurement, with the rubric and decision rule pinned in writing before scoring starts**.

**Concrete protocol I would run:**

1. **Lock the rubric and the decision rule in this document. Do not amend after scoring begins.**
2. **Run the sec.5 proxy (binary ship/no-ship on a v1-NOW slidelab-intro deck), score in one sitting, ~5 min.** Mark fabrication-flavored rejects with a flag.
3. **Apply the sec.4 decision rule mechanically to the recorded number.** Ambiguous band defaults to v2. No re-scoring; no "let me build another brief."
4. **Cross-check against the v2 smoke artifacts** (count SKELETON_REJECTED / FALLBACK_MERMAID events on slidelab-intro). If v2''s asymmetric refusal events fired on slides that v1 would have built fabricated-content for, v2 is justified categorically regardless of v1-NOW''s %.
5. **Hard stop: one scoring event. One number. One decision rule applied. Done in 30 minutes including write-up.**

**Single biggest risk if you ignore this:** Stage A produces a 50% number, Mario re-scores to "look more carefully," the rubric quietly drifts, and the decision is made on a number that no longer measures what it claimed to. That is worse than no measurement.

The discipline Stage A needs is the same discipline v2''s Hardline rules enforce on slide content: **refuse to fabricate the missing rigor.** If the protocol can''t decide on one scoring pass under a pre-committed rubric, the protocol is wrong — and the right answer is to apply the default-to-v2 rule and move on, not to keep scoring until the number resolves itself.
