# quote / dark-centered

**Family:** Quote

**What this is.** Full-bleed BRAND_PRIMARY canvas. Typography IS the visual — a large 28px italic white quote occupies center-of-body, attribution below in BRAND_ACCENT_SOFT. No cards, no chart, no image. The BRAND_ACCENT 56px opener rule above the quote is the sole accent moment.

**Use when.** The slide needs an emotional or authoritative moment of pause. A stakeholder voice, an expert's framing, a customer truth. One slide, one idea, complete whitespace discipline.

**What makes the structure work.**
- Dark canvas creates maximum contrast — white 28px text is loud without needing to shout.
- The opener rule (BRAND_ACCENT 56px wide, 4px tall) "opens" the quote zone — it's a visual runway before the text. This is the ONE accent moment; no other BRAND_ACCENT appears.
- Attribution at 14px BRAND_ACCENT_SOFT (below the body floor but its role is identity, not argument; 14px = the absolute floor, honored).
- Eyebrow "VOICE OF THE CLIENT" at 11px sits in the top-left — the only navigational text on an otherwise full-bleed dark canvas.
- Decorative open/close quote marks at 48px WHITE add visual depth without weight (they're not bold, not BRAND_ACCENT — pure scale play).

**Don't do this with this layout.**
- Don't use a light background — the entire point of this variant is the dark-canvas contrast.
- Don't add bullets or supporting points — the quote carries its own weight. If you need evidence, use a different layout family.
- Don't use two quotes on one dark slide — one voice, one idea.

**Structural delta from other Quote variants.**
- Dark-centered vs. pull-quote-with-rule: this variant has no vertical rule, no split panel, no photo. Purely typographic.
- Dark-centered vs. quote-on-light: inverse tonal register — one is intimate (light), this one is authoritative (dark).
